\section{Problem Statement}
As the society got advanced people get increasing need for a fast, economical and reliable transportation system which is both cheap and affordable. The urban commuters are confronted with various issues like lengthy waiting periods, high fares, congestion in the city streets, environmental issues due to excessive use of vehicles etc. These issues have never been so effectively solved with traditional modes of transport and existing ride-halters and it's clear that something new is needed.

The market is dominated by ride hailing solutions like Careem, Uber and Bykea, as well as intercity carpooling solutions BlaBlaCar, which also meets people's mobility needs. These services are drivers of change in the way people travel, but with significant shortcomings. Typical ride-haling services typically send drivers to pick up riders at certain spots, where they are likely to be forced off their routes. The consequences of this are lengthy delays, more fuel spent, more money collected by the passengers and less for the drivers once the operating costs are taken into account.

A significant problem is also inadequate mechanisms for share of expenses. Unlike most platforms, most services do not pay special attention to matching people when they are on parallel journeys so to decrease transportation costs. Cultural considerations are also ignored often, especially gender preferences that are significant in such areas of Pakistan. Moreover, additional pickups delay and increase vehicle cost, environmental effects are still serious as there are more vehicles on road means more pollution and congestion.

These problems impact on everyday commuters who require affordable transportation, drivers who would like to look for out options to turn away from unnecessary maneuvers to do business and the broader community that suffers from pushing street traffic.Those are problems for everyday commuters who need affordable transportation, drivers who would prefer to search for methods to deviate from pointless route changes, and the general public which suffers from traffic congestion and air pollution. These are not trivial issues, but rather factors that contribute to low efficiency and low utilization of sustainable transit opportunities.

\section{Proposed Solution Overview}

The Lets Go Ride Sharing App is a ride sharing mobile application by which any passenger and driver who travels along similar route will be connected to get the ride, thereby changing the way commuting works in local communities. Unlike other traditional services that dispatch drivers to random destinations, Lets Go Ride Sharing App pairs people who are already travelling in the same direction, and share the trip and expenses.

The envisioned system tackles with the identified problems by making the following innovations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Route Based Matching:} Drivers set up specific roads they want to drive; users search to match specific road they want to be driven on without making any extra unnecessary trips.

    \item \textbf{Addressing Cultural Norms:} Gender-based preferences enable users to select drivers they are comfortable with, which is not typically found in global platforms.

    \item \textbf{Hybrid Pricing:} Best fare recommendationswill be made by the app for distance and fuel cost and the driver/car owner and passenger have full freedom to adjust the suggested fare then agree with each other to the final value.

    \item \textbf{Safety Stack:} Trust between users is created through identity document verification, real-time location tracking, pickup codes and SOS emergency features.
\end{itemize}

Incorporating machine learning algorithms to promise personalized recommendations and a chatbot for prompt assistance, the Lets Go Ride Sharing App provides an environmentally friendly, user-friendly, and budget-friendly solution for contemporary commuters.

\section{Scope of the System}

The Lets Go Ride Sharing App application targets local communities such as office complexes, university campuses, and residential neighborhoods to provide eco-friendly and cost-effective commuting solutions. The system consists of the following components:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{User Registration:} A fully integrated user registration process with complete document verification, including CNIC, vehicle registration, and driver's license documents for drivers.

    \item \textbf{Route Specification:} Drivers specify exact routes on the map, including start points, intermediate stops, and destinations using an interactive map interface; passengers choose pickup and drop-off locations from those predefined stops.

    \item \textbf{Real-Time Location:} Continuous location updates provide arrival notifications, driver location, and ride progress information to both drivers and passengers during active rides.

    \item \textbf{Gender-Based Preferences:} Passengers can filter available rides by driver gender to match their personal comfort and cultural preferences.

    \item \textbf{Flexible Fare Agreement:} The system supports automatic fare suggestion and allows both parties to adjust and agree on the final fare before the ride begins.

    \item \textbf{Payment Methods:} Cash payments and manual bank transfers are supported, including QR-based bank transfer where applicable, followed by manual two-step confirmation in which the passenger marks the payment and the driver confirms receipt.

    \item \textbf{Notification System:} In-built notifications and reviews help maintain quality and strengthen community trust.

    \item \textbf{Safety Features:} Emergency contact integration, manual document verification, live location tracking, and SOS alerts support user safety.

    \item \textbf{Administrative Dashboard:} A web-based dashboard supports user verification, dispute resolution, financial analytics, and account management.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Out of Scope:} Automated payment gateways, intercity long-distance trips, non-Android mobile platforms (with iOS deferred to future releases), and real-time automated identity verification are excluded from the current scope. All verification remains admin-manual.

\section{System Architecture and Modules}

The Lets Go Ride Sharing App system follows a \textbf{three-tier client-server architecture} with clear separation of concerns that ensures scalability, maintainability, and independent module deployment. The four-layer view used in the software design chapter is a detailed decomposition of this three-tier model, with external services separated explicitly.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Presentation Tier:} Cross-platform Flutter mobile applications for passengers and drivers provide consistent interfaces with role-switching capabilities. The administrative web console uses HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript to provide dashboard access for user verification and dispute management.

    \item \textbf{Application Tier:} Django backend services provide JSON-based APIs for authorization, ride lifecycle management, bookings, fare negotiation, chat, live tracking, pickup code verification, payment confirmations, dispute management, SOS alerts, and analytics. Periodic HTTP updates are used to deliver near real-time behavior on mobile devices.

    \item \textbf{Data Tier:} PostgreSQL, via Supabase, serves as the core transactional database. Secure object storage is used for document uploads such as CNIC images, driving license images, and vehicle photographs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Module 1: Profile Management (Registration \& Verification)}

This module manages user registration, authentication, and profile management for both passengers and drivers. It captures the user's essential details such as name, phone number, email, postal address, CNIC, driving license, and vehicle registration information. The module uses two-factor authentication with OTP sent to email and phone, and supports role management between driver and passenger modes. All uploaded documents are stored securely and linked to the user's record for administrative verification.

\textbf{Functional Requirements:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FE-1.1:} The system shall allow users to register by entering name, username, email, password, address, phone number, CNIC, and gender, with validation for unique username and email.

    \item \textbf{FE-1.2:} The system shall support uploading profile pictures, live photos, CNIC front and back images, and license images with validation for size (less than 1MB) and format (JPG/PNG).

    \item \textbf{FE-1.3:} The system shall generate and send OTPs to both email and phone within 5 minutes of registration submission, and OTPs shall expire after 5 minutes.

    \item \textbf{FE-1.4:} The system shall change account status to "Pending Admin Verification" after successful OTP verification and shall not allow feature access until admin approval.

    \item \textbf{FE-1.5:} The system shall enforce business rules requiring a bank account number and bank name to be provided together if either is entered.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Module 2: Communication Module (Chat \& Notifications)}

\textbf{Description:} This module enables seamless communication between drivers and passengers through secure one-to-one messaging and real-time notifications. After booking confirmation, users can coordinate pickup details through in-app chat. The notification system delivers alerts for booking requests, acceptances, rejections, cancellations, payment confirmations, reminders, and emergency events. Communication records are retained for audit and dispute resolution purposes.

\textbf{Functional Requirements:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FE-2.1:} The system shall enable secure one-to-one messaging between drivers and passengers only after booking confirmation.

    \item \textbf{FE-2.2:} The system shall allow drivers to broadcast messages to accepted passengers from the ride management screen.

    \item \textbf{FE-2.3:} The system shall generate real-time push notifications for booking requests, acceptances, rejections, cancellations, and payment confirmations.

    \item \textbf{FE-2.4:} The system shall maintain immutable chat logs with timestamps for all messages, linked to the associated booking for audit purposes.

    \item \textbf{FE-2.5:} The system shall support multiple notification channels including in-app push, SMS, and email with configurable user preferences.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Module 3: Core Functional Module (Ride Management \& Booking)}

\textbf{Description:} This module delivers the main benefit of the Lets Go Ride Sharing App system by allowing drivers to create rides and passengers to discover and book them. Drivers use an interactive map editor to mark start points, intermediate stops, and destinations, with automatic place name suggestions from map providers. Ride parameters include date and time, vehicle selection, available seats, gender preferences, negotiation flags, and fare settings. Passengers browse available rides through route preview cards showing times, fares, and driver details, then book by selecting pickup and drop-off stops and seat counts. The module uses seat locking during booking requests to prevent overbooking.

\textbf{Functional Requirements:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FE-3.1:} The system shall provide a map-based route editor where drivers can mark start (green), intermediate (orange), and end (red) stops with auto-fetched place names.

    \item \textbf{FE-3.2:} The system shall calculate and display auto-fare based on total route distance, vehicle type, and fuel coefficient, while allowing driver override with confirmation.

    \item \textbf{FE-3.3:} The system shall display ride cards to passengers showing route preview, departure time, available seats, driver rating, gender preference, and fare.

    \item \textbf{FE-3.4:} The system shall allow passengers to request booking by selecting pickup and drop-off stops from the driver's route, specifying seat counts with gender-based allocation.

    \item \textbf{FE-3.5:} The system shall lock selected seats for a configurable reservation window of 5 minutes during booking request processing to prevent double-booking.

    \item \textbf{FE-3.6:} The system shall notify drivers of booking requests and allow them to accept or reject requests within a configurable timeframe.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Module 4: Management / Admin Module}

\textbf{Description:} This web-based administrative panel gives administrators complete control over platform operations. The module displays paginated user lists with document viewing capabilities and supports efficient verification workflows. Administrators can verify, reject, or ban users, and CNIC blacklisting prevents re-registration of banned individuals. The dispute management interface presents cases with evidence such as chat logs, GPS traces, pickup code attempts, and uploaded images, allowing resolution with notes. The module also provides financial analytics, usage reports, and configuration management for platform policies.

\textbf{Functional Requirements:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FE-4.1:} The system shall provide an admin dashboard displaying paginated user lists with filtering by verification status and document viewing capabilities.

    \item \textbf{FE-4.2:} The system shall allow administrators to verify users, reject users with a reason, or ban users by CNIC, with CNIC blacklisting preventing future registration attempts.

    \item \textbf{FE-4.3:} The system shall flag user verifications pending longer than 24 hours and surface them to admin dashboards for expedited handling.

    \item \textbf{FE-4.4:} The system shall display disputes with comprehensive evidence including chat transcripts, GPS location traces, pickup code attempt logs, and uploaded images.

    \item \textbf{FE-4.5:} The system shall allow administrators to resolve disputes with notes, update dispute status, and notify the involved parties of the resolution outcome.

    \item \textbf{FE-4.6:} The system shall generate system usage reports including ride statistics, user growth metrics, and financial summaries.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Module 5: Advanced / Additional Features (ML \& Chatbot)}

\textbf{Description:} This module delivers intelligent capabilities that improve user experience through personalization and automated support. The machine learning component analyzes user travel history, saved routes, search behavior, and preferences to generate personalized ride recommendations that appear on the home screen. Using content-based filtering, it computes similarity scores between user profiles and available rides and ranks results by relevance. The chatbot, powered by Generative AI, provides instant support by answering FAQs from the knowledge base and assisting with app navigation. For ride recommendation queries, it interfaces with the ML module to present personalized suggestions conversationally.

\textbf{Functional Requirements:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FE-5.1:} The system shall analyze user travel history, saved routes, recent searches, and preferences to generate personalized ride recommendations using content-based filtering algorithms.

    \item \textbf{FE-5.2:} The system shall rank recommended rides using similarity scores based on route coordinates, departure time proximity, driver rating, and price alignment with user history.

    \item \textbf{FE-5.3:} The system shall respect driver-set gender preferences and safety filters in all recommendations, never showing rides to disallowed genders.

    \item \textbf{FE-5.4:} The system shall fall back to deterministic rule-based ranking based on distance, departure time, and driver rating when the ML service is unavailable.

    \item \textbf{FE-5.5:} The system shall provide a chatbot interface that answers frequently asked questions by accessing the knowledge base and user manual content.

    \item \textbf{FE-5.6:} The chatbot shall provide personalized ride recommendations when requested by calling the recommendation engine and presenting results conversationally.

    \item \textbf{FE-5.7:} The system shall log all chatbot interactions for analytics, continuous improvement, and identification of gaps in automated support.
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions and Dependencies}

The design and development of the Lets Go Ride Sharing App system is based on the following assumptions and dependencies:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{User Participation:} The system assumes a critical mass of active users for efficient ride matching; a small user base may limit ride twinning effectiveness and reduce available ride options.

    \item \textbf{Third-Party Services:} The system depends on external mapping APIs for route calculation, geocoding, and ETA estimation, as well as LLM APIs for chatbot functionality. Service downtime, API changes, or rate limitations may temporarily affect application functionality.

    \item \textbf{Internet Connectivity:} The system requires stable internet connectivity and accurate GPS data. Users in areas with poor network coverage or inaccurate GPS signals may experience degraded performance or inability to use core features.

    \item \textbf{Device Capabilities:} The system assumes users have Android devices running version 8.0 or higher with GPS, camera functionality, and at least 2GB RAM. Device fragmentation may require additional testing efforts.

    \item \textbf{Notification Delivery:} The system depends on Firebase Cloud Messaging for push notifications and SMS/email gateways for alerts. Delivery reliability depends on these third-party services, with retry mechanisms implemented for transient failures.

    \item \textbf{Manual Verification:} The system depends on administrator review for document verification. Thorough verification may slow registration but improves platform safety and trust.

    \item \textbf{Payment Processing:} The system assumes manual payment confirmation without an integrated payment gateway. Payment reconciliation requires administrative oversight and user honesty in marking payments.

    \item \textbf{Data Retention:} The system assumes compliance with data protection regulations and platform store policies regarding storage of user documents and personal information.
\end{itemize}
